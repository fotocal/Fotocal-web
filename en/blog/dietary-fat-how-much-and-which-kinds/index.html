Dietary Fat: How Much You Need and Which Kinds to Choose

>

Fat isn’t fattening by default — it’s essential. Learn how much dietary fat you need, the difference between fat types, and how to choose wisely.

>
June 18, 2026 > · 6 min read · Fotocal team

For years, “fat-free” was the health halo of choice. We now know dietary fat is essential: it builds cell membranes, produces hormones, and helps absorb vitamins A, D, E and K. The goal isn’t to fear fat — it’s to use it well.

How much fat do you need?

A common range is 20–35% of total calories. Going much lower can disrupt hormones; going very high simply crowds out other foods, since fat is calorie-dense at nine calories per gram. Portion awareness matters more than avoidance.

The types of fat

  • Unsaturated (the good ones): olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, fatty fish. Support heart health.
  • Saturated: fine in moderation — butter, red meat, coconut. Keep it reasonable, not zero.
  • Trans fats: avoid. Found in some processed and fried foods; genuinely harmful.

Don’t forget omega-3s

Omega-3 fats, found in salmon, sardines, walnuts and flax, are anti-inflammatory and support brain and heart health. Most people don’t get enough, so aim for oily fish a couple of times a week.

Fat and satiety

Fat slows digestion and adds flavor, which helps meals feel satisfying. But because it’s so calorie-dense, a heavy hand with oil or nut butter can quietly blow your calorie target — this is where measuring pays off.

Because fats hide a lot of calories in small amounts, logging them accurately matters. Fotocal estimates the fat on your plate so a generous drizzle of oil doesn’t go uncounted.

Key takeaways

  • Fat is essential — aim for roughly 20–35% of calories.
  • Favor unsaturated fats; keep saturated moderate; avoid trans fats.
  • Include omega-3 sources like oily fish.
  • Measure fats carefully — they’re calorie-dense.
