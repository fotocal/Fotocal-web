Protein Timing: Does It Actually Matter?

>

Is the “anabolic window” real? Learn what the science says about protein timing, the 30-minute post-workout myth, and what actually matters.

>
March 8, 2026 > · 5 min read · Fotocal team

Few fitness ideas are as sticky as the “anabolic window” — the belief that you must consume protein within 30 minutes of training or your workout is wasted. It sells a lot of supplements. It’s also largely overblown. Here’s what the evidence actually supports.

Total daily protein comes first

By a wide margin, the most important factor for building and keeping muscle is how much protein you eat across the whole day, not the exact minute you eat it. Hit your daily target (1.6–2.2g per kg) and you’ve handled 90% of what matters.

The window is wider than you think

Research shows the post-workout “window” spans several hours, not 30 minutes. If you ate a protein-containing meal a few hours before training, the amino acids are still circulating afterward. There’s no need to panic-drink a shake in the locker room.

Where distribution helps a little

Spreading protein across three or four meals of roughly 25–40g each appears slightly better for muscle than getting it all in one or two meals. This is a minor optimization on top of the daily total, not a rule to stress over.

Practical takeaways

  • Prioritize your total daily protein above all.
  • Spread it across several meals if convenient.
  • Eating protein sometime around training is fine — the window is hours wide.
  • Don’t let timing anxiety distract from the big picture.

Key takeaways

  • Total daily protein matters far more than timing.
  • The post-workout window is hours, not 30 minutes.
  • Spreading protein across meals is a small bonus.
